# Evaluate labs of interest

Produce a decision aid, not an endorsement or job application. Confirm user-specific priorities from the current conversation and adapt the comparison to the candidate. If context is missing, state a modest assumption rather than inventing career history. The default comparison for this user's current search is a programmer with established scRNA-seq, spatial and wet-lab experience, seeking overseas clinical/disease-relevant skills, with a potential later return to Singapore.

## Screen before reporting

Internally check disease context; integrated wet and computational work in the same PI-associated programme; active independent lab; a **verified** international/overseas-trained trainee who led a first-author primary study with this PI senior or corresponding; and no report on this lab in the preceding ten weeks. Keep the ten-week check out of the report and user-facing evidence bundle. Consult the prior-report ledger only when needed to clear a real run. Do not infer foreign training from someone's name. For actual overseas searches, enforce the user's geography rule. If a gate fails or is genuinely unverified, find the next lab in a discovery run. Four visible criteria receive one-to-five-star evidence-strength scores, distinct from internal eligibility: disease context, wet/dry integration, active independent lab, and overseas-trained first-author trainee. Explain the score through the substantive sections, with no note or footnote below the criteria table. A requested worked example may use an otherwise ineligible lab; mention the example status only where relevant to the substantive evaluation.

## Gather evidence

1. Resolve the PI, current institution and independent appointment from official pages. Describe the institute's clinical partnerships, relevant facilities and training context in a page-1 section. Search recent PubMed papers and journal primary sources. Confirm author order and correspondence from the paper, not search-result snippets. Treat middle authorship, lab-wide announcements and consortium memberships as collaboration until ownership is established.
2. Select 5–8 *distinct original papers* representing the PI's recent methods and research strands. Prefer last/corresponding-author papers at the current lab; include older foundational work when necessary and label it. Exclude reviews, preprints and corrections from the peer-reviewed method denominator. Explain any co-led collaborator-dependent study. Do not count a correction as a second paper.
3. Read methods or full-text source and assign explicit paper codes to method families. A paper contributes at most one to each family, regardless of replicate assays. Use a lower bound when only a subset is verified. Distinguish newly generated sequencing from reanalysis; histology from spatial omics; author affiliation from hands-on access. Prioritize techniques repeated in at least two selected papers, list meaningful one-offs separately, and show the denominator.
4. Verify the trainee's prior training location using a CV, institutional biography or the trainee's own published record; verify first-author primary study and this PI's senior ownership independently. Note the trainee's outcome and when it occurred. Qualify mentorship, lab size, funding runway and postdoc autonomy when public evidence cannot establish them.
5. Trace alumni **who have PhDs**, prioritizing former postdocs and research fellows as the most relevant comparators for this applicant. Search for every named PhD-qualified person who worked in the lab in the preceding five years, including doctoral trainees who later left, and record first-author work when established. For each, distinguish the verified lab role, years or date of membership, later career position and evidence date. Give former PhD-qualified fellows visible space on page 1; keep the fullest verified roster on page 2. Label PI-supervised PhD graduates who never held a postdoctoral lab role separately. Exclude current staff and students, and do not infer a departure from silence. Compare directory, thesis, graduation, lab archive and destination-institution records. If only a partial roster or outcomes can be checked, say so prominently and name the unverified outcomes; do not claim completeness from a publicity count.
6. Make the sections after Methods answer distinct questions. **Research ownership** links selected work to the PI and shows whether a trainee led a substantive experiment. **Alumni outcomes** gives named later positions for PhD-qualified former fellows. **Postdoc fit** evaluates marginal skills, clinical relevance, career geography and PI environment for this applicant. Do not repeat a paper summary as career advice, or append a verdict that restates the alumni list. Interpret methods frequency as evidence of work done, not a promise of individual training access.
7. State **Research directions** in two explicitly labelled strands: the PI/lab's **stated research directions** from a public institutional description, and the **direction inferred from the recent original papers**. Cite both. Interpret an inference as a working research trajectory, not a promised active project. Avoid repeated generic caveats about public-data uncertainty and non-exhaustive coverage; express any specific missing outcome or role once where it changes the assessment.
8. Estimate the lab's common working language in one or two sentences immediately after Institute. Prefer an explicit lab statement; otherwise infer from relevant institutional language requirements and verified international trainees, citing that evidence. Label it as an estimate when inferred.

For detailed input fields and an example method mapping, read [input-schema.md](references/input-schema.md).

## Make the document

Create a structured JSON evidence file, then run:

```bash
python3 scripts/render_report.py evidence.json output/lab_report
```

The script uses `assets/report.tex.tpl` and writes `.tex` plus a compiled `.pdf`. Keep the *assessment* on page 1 in two columns **with sequential flow: fill the left column first and continue in the right**. Use `multicols*`, never a forced `\columnbreak` or column balancing. Page 1's section order is **Criteria → Institute → Working language → Research directions → Methods → Research ownership → Alumni outcomes → Postdoc fit**. Page 2 holds full method counts, the fuller alumni outcomes and citations. It rejects an extra report page. Use the plain LaTeX theme and set every section heading larger than the page's paragraph text, even if the body size changes. Keep title and full-width institute subtitle free of top-right labels. Do not print a top decision banner, ten-week history, or notes or asterisk footnotes under `Criteria`. In every research-paper reference, name the actual last author and describe the target PI's corresponding/senior role. No unexplained first-author-only citations.

When original open-access paper PDFs can be retrieved, locate and visually verify the *one source-PDF page showing Figure 1*. Supply each PDF path and one-based source page to `figures` in the JSON. The script appends pages after TeX compilation, checks that Figure 1 text is present, and creates source-page bookmarks; never embed article pages in the LaTeX. Cite the DOI in the references. Say which figure pages could not be obtained. Do not fabricate a paper page from an HTML figure or attach an entire article. Respect source licenses and credit the article.

Render the finished PDF page 1 and page 2 for visual inspection; check for clipped text, legibility, unintended third report page, and that appended pages show Figure 1. Fix and rerun as needed. Return the PDF and TeX; include the evidence JSON when it aids reuse. Save deliverables using the normal file workflow. Do not start a recurring schedule unless the user requests one.
