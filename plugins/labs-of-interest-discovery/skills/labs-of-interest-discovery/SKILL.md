# Discover labs of interest

Produce an auditable **institute → recent senior-author primary paper → disease → wet+dry** lab shortlist. The default scope is the 37 institutes in [references/predefined-institute-list.md](references/predefined-institute-list.md), no Singapore institution, and the seven calendar dates ending on the execution date in the user's time zone. Follow an explicitly supplied predefined list or date window instead. State the exact inclusive range and search timestamp.

## Discover and date

1. Use PubMed E-utilities as the core discovery source. Run `python3 scripts/pubmed_scan.py --as-of YYYY-MM-DD --output records.json` from this skill directory or pass its absolute path. The script queries publication date and broad affiliation aliases, pages through *all* PubMed IDs, fetches XML in batches, rate-limits requests, and stores author affiliations, publication types, abstracts and distinct publication dates. Use the date in the user's time zone, not an implicit machine date. A NCBI API key is optional via `NCBI_API_KEY`.
2. Inspect all retrieved records, not just the first promising results. Search by additional name variants and senior-author names when a predefined-list entry yields unexpectedly few hits. Affiliation queries match **any author**, so a raw hit is not an eligible PI. Record per-institute coverage, false matches, and the exact search strings or script version used.
3. PubMed's publication-date search also matches issue dates. Prefer the earliest verifiable publication of the *peer-reviewed article*: compare `ArticleDate` and `PubMedPubDate` (use only `epublish`, not indexing dates), journal issue, publisher version history, Crossref, or Europe PMC. Exclude a paper published online before the week despite an in-window issue date. Manually resolve records with only a print date or incomplete date; do not silently drop them or assert that their issue date was their first publication. Distinguish an old preprint from a newly published peer-reviewed article. Note indexing lag and consider a rerun.
4. Screen original biological/clinical research. Reject reviews, perspectives, commentaries, news, corrections, conference abstracts, case reports without a substantive research analysis, bare protocols, and method/resource reports with no meaningful biological or disease application. PubMed's `Journal Article` type is **provisional**, not proof of primary research. Maintain a one-row-per-PMID decision ledger with date, article type, senior affiliation, disease, wet, dry and exclusion reason. Count records at each gate from that ledger; report both broad retrieval count and **verified** primary-paper count, never relabel a coarse publication-type count as verified primary research.

## Attribute the lab

5. Start with the last author. Include a documented co-senior author only if a paper's author note or equivalent explicitly says so; check whether co-seniors lead distinct groups. Confirm each candidate's **current active** lab using an official institutional page. A genuine concurrent affiliation with a predefined-list institute is enough even if another is elsewhere, but a past affiliation, distant consortium partner, named core facility, hospital-only coauthor, or similarly named institute in another city is not enough. No umbrella substitution (RIKEN, Max Planck Society, CNRS, etc.). Treat an actual research scientist who is last author differently from the officially named group leader; identify the responsible group without inventing an independent PI.
6. Apply the disease gate to the *study's experiments or analysis*: cancer; infections; autoimmune/inflammatory, fibrotic, cardiovascular, neurological, metabolic or genetic disease; pathological aging, injury, repair or degeneration. Generic future medical relevance does not pass. For a nonpathogenic surrogate used for disease-oriented drug discovery, state exactly what pathogen or disease **was not tested** and explain the boundary decision. A cancer cell line used solely as a convenient generic model does not itself establish a cancer question.
7. Assess wet and dry **for the discovery paper first**. Wet can be primary material, perturbation, culture, model organisms, organoids, molecular assays or substantive imaging. Dry must contribute real omics integration, modelling, genomics, machine learning, statistical genetics or analysis beyond routine statistics, differential-expression software alone or routine image quantification. Trace author contributions, methods and institutional roles: sequencing outsourced to a core, an independent computational co-senior, or someone else's validation may leave lab-level integration uncertain. If ambiguous, inspect the group's official page and recent primary papers for repeated integration.
8. Label **Strong** when paper/lab clearly integrates substantial wet and dry work; **Moderate** when both matter but responsibility is distributed or the same-lab programme is plausible without full confirmation; **Insufficient** when either component or lab-level link cannot be supported. Only Strong or Moderate passes. A wholly computational discovery paper from an otherwise mixed-method lab belongs in near misses under this paper-led funnel unless new paper-level wet evidence changes the assessment.
9. De-duplicate by responsible lab; combine all qualifying papers under that lab. Do not count one shared unit twice simply because two authors jointly supervised a paper. Do not rank or filter by journal metrics.

## Report and audit

Start with exact date range, predefined-list size, raw PubMed hit count, verified primary-paper count, disease survivors, wet+dry survivors and unique labs. If a count is only provisional, label it separately and explain why. Present every passing lab in a table with **PI/lab, institute, exact paper title and direct PMID/DOI link, actual publication date, disease context, wet evidence, dry evidence, Strong/Moderate label, and why it passed**. Follow with a short evidence note for each lab whose integration or attribution needs explanation.

Include a compact **Near misses** section for plausible disease-relevant papers rejected on wet+dry attribution, senior-institute identity, primary-research status, indirect disease context or first-publication date. Link their PMIDs and name the reason. End with **Pipeline observations** on affiliation ambiguity, indexing/date errors, collaborations and likely false negatives. Mark uncertainty explicitly rather than fabricating a verdict. Cite the primary paper plus institutional or publisher sources where they carry the decision. Never stop after several good-looking labs.
